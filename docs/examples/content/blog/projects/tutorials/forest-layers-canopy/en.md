The canopy is the uppermost continuous layer of foliage in a woodland — the ceiling of the forest. In a mature temperate forest it sits between 15 and 30 metres above the ground, formed by the crowns of the dominant trees: oak, beech, ash, lime, or sycamore depending on soil and region.
