The understorey is the layer of woody vegetation growing between the canopy and the ground — typically between one and ten metres high. It includes shade-tolerant shrubs like hazel, hawthorn, blackthorn, elder, and holly, as well as young trees waiting for a canopy gap to release them upward.

The berry-producing shrubs of the understorey — hawthorn, elder, blackthorn, holly — are critical food sources for migrant thrushes in autumn. The dense thorn thickets provide nesting habitat for nightingale, garden warbler, and whitethroat. Dormice depend on hazel nuts as a pre-hibernation food store, and their presence is one of the strongest indicators of understorey quality.
